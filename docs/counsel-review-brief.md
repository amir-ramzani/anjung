# Brief for counsel: PDPA review (plan P5-10)

Purpose: get written sign-off, or a tracked list of actions, on the points below before any real tenant data is loaded. This is a briefing, not legal advice. Facts come from the code (API contract v0.14.0) and `docs/data-classification.md`.

## The system in one paragraph
Anjung is a rental-management web service for Malaysian landlords. Landlords (organisations) manage properties, tenants, leases, rent invoices, payments and maintenance. Tenants log in to see and pay their invoices and report repairs. Each organisation's data is isolated from the others. Inside an organisation, a property manager sees only the properties they manage and the tenants linked to them, and an administrator can switch off a staff member's access at once or limit it to dates. Tenants and staff get their login through a one-time link to set their password, sent in a welcome email (or, for tenants, on WhatsApp). Before a lease starts, each tenant accepts the tenancy agreement in the portal by typing their name, or staff record that they signed on paper. Hosted on AWS in Malaysia (`ap-southeast-5`, decision D4). Card and bank details are never stored (a payment gateway hosts them; gateway not yet chosen, D2).

## What the system already does (for context)
- Export of all personal data held on a person (`GET /v1/privacy/export`, staff: `GET /v1/tenants/{id}/privacy-export`); a test fails if a personal entity is missing from it. It includes the tenant's own signing evidence and never names co-tenants.
- Erasure by anonymisation: personal fields overwritten, financial records kept without personal fields, documents under retention hold kept under a neutral name, job is idempotent and has a dry run. Refused while the tenant has an active or draft lease.
- Consent and withdrawal with history; notification preferences per channel.
- Encryption at rest (KMS) for the table and documents; transport TLS; role-based access; audit log of staff actions with IP.
- No personal data in logs, traces, metrics or error messages (tracing tests check exception messages are not recorded).
- Tenant isolation tested route by route.
- Need-to-know access inside an organisation: a property manager sees only the properties they manage and the tenants created by them or renting there. Anything else behaves as if it did not exist. Tested route by route.
- Company plan: Anjung sets each landlord's unit limit and end date. When a plan ends, the landlord's staff can only view and export for 30 days while tenants can still see and pay their bills; after that nobody from that landlord, tenants included, can sign in until it is renewed. Question for counsel: while closed, tenants cannot reach their own records in the app, so access requests would need to go through Anjung (processor) or the landlord (data user).
- Branding: each landlord company can show its own name, registration number, logo and contact details on its sign-in page, in emails ("<company> via Anjung", replies go to the company) and on receipts. These details are public on its sign-in page. For a landlord who is an individual, the contact details would be their own personal data, which they choose to publish.
- Parking bays can be let with an optional vehicle plate number. It is shown to staff and the tenants on that lease only, included in the tenant's export, and removed on erasure.
- Invitations: a set-password link works once, expires after 7 days, is replaced by any newer link, and is stored only as a fingerprint. Wrong, used and expired links all get the same answer. When the app sends it, staff never see it.
- Deletion in every module: deactivate (reversible) or delete. Delete is shown first and either removes a record made by mistake completely (a tenant who never had a lease loses everything, including their login and identity files), erases a tenant with history through a privacy request, or is refused with the reason (live lease, money owed either way). Every delete needs a reason, kept in the audit log.
- Staff access can be switched off, or limited to dates, and it takes effect on the next request, not when the login expires.
- Tenancy agreements: each acceptance is recorded with the time, the document, the IP address and the browser, and the audit log keeps a separate entry. Changing any term after sending clears the acceptances, so nobody is held to terms they did not see. Signed agreements are held under the retention hold and cannot be deleted.

## What is not done (so counsel is not surprised)
**Demo setup:** a home-network demo copy runs with a password-free test sign-in and demo data only. It must never hold real tenant data, and it is not the system under review.

Also not done: the retention purge stays off until the periods are set (question 4); the app cannot yet send WhatsApp messages itself (provider not chosen, D3), so for now WhatsApp invitations are sent by staff from their own phones; no retention purge; no privacy notice text; no DPA; no DPO named; no penetration test yet; no tabletop of the breach process; Malay translations are machine-written and unreviewed; deployed controls (WAF, GuardDuty, CloudTrail) exist as code only until deployment.
